# Draft resolution - Week 11 safety app

**Recommendation:** Option C, don't run it. Verdict label: DON'T DO IT, because "don't run it" is itself the top scoring option (see scoring rules).

**Why, in one paragraph:** A fails on every pillar at once. B as written, published error rates and an appeal path in one line, specifies nothing: no rates by group, no base rate test, no independent audit, no named accountable official, no right for a flagged person to inspect the evidence, no limit on data taken or kept. The room's disclosure seat (Lu) conceded that an underspecified safeguard is how secrecy survives a transparency vote, and the room's mechanics seat (Sumpter) could produce no base rate at which B passes. B as fully specified is a different proposal, and it is carried below as the revisit path, not as a reason to run anything now.

## Conditions (for any future reconsideration, all checkable)

1. Error rates published by group before any pilot, worked against real base rates, with a pre-set threshold: true flags must not be outnumbered by false flags.
2. Independent audit access to the system and its test data. A vendor report does not count.
3. A named accountable official for the system, published, who must justify a flag; the burden does not sit on the flagged person.
4. A flagged person, or their reviewer, can inspect the evidence behind the flag, under protective order if needed.
5. No behavioral surplus: collection limited to a named task, retention limited and stated, no entry travels to other services without a fresh, stated reason, and a flagged person can have a wrong entry erased.
6. A showing that exposure falls first for the most exposed, and that a person can fail to match their file and survive the mismatch without cost.
