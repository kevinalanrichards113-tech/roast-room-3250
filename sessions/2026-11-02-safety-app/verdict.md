## Why the council landed here

- The arithmetic fails in the app's own terms: rare events at city scale mean false flags are expected to swamp true flags, and no confusion matrix was produced (Sumpter, The data manifesto / audit stance).
- B as written specifies nothing: no rates by group, no audit, no named official, no right to inspect a flag. The disclosure seat conceded an underspecified safeguard is how secrecy survives a transparency vote (Lu, The three opacities).
- Accuracy would not settle ownership or politics: the app takes behavioral surplus beyond any safety task and encodes suspicion as standing governance (Zuboff, Behavioral surplus; Golumbia, Software as politics).
- The cost of error lands on the flagged, as exposure and as a debt of proof, before any appeal begins (Butler, Frames and grievability; Graeber, Primordial debt).

## Conditions

Any future reconsideration must meet all six on paper first. Meeting them earns a new council, not a pilot.

1. Error rates by group before any pilot, against real base rates, pre-set threshold: true flags not outnumbered by false flags.
2. Independent audit access. A vendor report does not count.
3. A named accountable official, published, who must justify a flag.
4. A flagged person, or their reviewer, can inspect the evidence behind the flag.
5. No behavioral surplus: collection and retention limited and stated, no entry travels without a fresh reason, wrong entries erasable.
6. Exposure falls first for the most exposed, and failing to match a file carries no cost.
